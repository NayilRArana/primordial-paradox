## Read this before the numbers

The critic is a rough critic and the tool says so in every review it writes: it cannot count pixels reliably, it misreads small text, it does not know this project's palette, and it will describe things that are not in the frame. Every picture here is a video frame, so the pixels carry H.264 ringing: measured, the four fight screens hold 362 to 465 distinct colours at 0.000 to 0.034 exact palette membership, while the flat cards hold 50 to 106 colours at 0.94 to 0.99. Those fight numbers are compression plus anti-aliased game art, and the report never turns a sheet level measurement into a claim about one frame.

Three limits, stated as limits:

1. Sprite seconds are an upper bound. A sprite is credited with the whole duration of every screen whose script or data file draws it, because per frame visibility is not measurable from 1 fps video. Screen seconds are exact: each of the 176 frames is named by its nearest captured screen and the counts sum to 176.
2. The critic scored contact sheets, so its own measured lines describe the sheet (for example 1048 x 1472 pixels, 186 colours on sheet_08) and not one frame. Per frame colour counts in section 3 come from the frames themselves.
3. Two captured frames are not ranked, with the reason in section 5: `sam_mask.png` (a diagnostic mask, not a screen) and `crawl_last_card.png` (0 seconds by this measurement).
