# Baroque engine research: corpus targets, Harmony of Dissonance devices, and the model question

Card `t_0ed3bd3f`, written 2026-10-26 by the research lane. The machine twins of this document are
`data/counterpoint_targets.json` (the numbers M1b generates against), `data/hod_dissonance_devices.json`
(the dissonance rules M1a can adopt), `tools/counterpoint/corpus_stats.py` (the measurement itself) and
`tools/counterpoint/check_counterpoint_targets.py` (the validation, which passes).

The generated tables sit in section 1 and section 2 and are written by the tool, not typed by hand, so
no number here can drift from the file M1b reads. Everything after section 2 is reading and judgement.

## The answer in six lines

1. The corpus is 35 public-domain Bach works and 17,130 notes: 15 two-part inventions, 15 three-part
   sinfonias and 5 chorale settings, all from the Mutopia Project, measured from files that live
   outside the repository with their URL, licence and SHA-256 on record.
2. A line that sounds written sits near: 0.585 of moves by step, 0.415 by leap, only 0.148 of moves a
   leap of a seventh or more, and 0.316 of leaps recovered by a step in the other direction.
3. The corpus is built on repetition, not on new material: 36.95 notes per 100 belong to a sequence
   that repeats at a new pitch. A generator that never sequences will sound unlike Bach long before
   any interval is wrong.
4. An answer enters most often 8 beats after the subject (26.4 percent of matched cells), then 6 beats
   (12.5), 3 beats (9.8) and 2 beats (9.6), transposed to the fifth 18.5 percent of the time, the fourth
   17.1 percent, and at the octave or unison 54.7 percent. Those are the canon distances a generator
   can start from.
5. Cadences land 11.04 times per 100 bars, and the commonest reading is the imperfect authentic (47.5
   percent), then perfect authentic (14.7), plagal (13.9) and deceptive (11.5). A cue needs a cadence
   roughly every nine bars, which is the phrase length the ear is listening for.
6. Harmony of Dissonance is not looser than Bach in how it resolves: it clashes 20.31 times per 100
   notes against Bach's 3.33, puts 82.5 percent of its beats on a clashing sonority against 11.7, and
   yet resolves 56.8 percent of its clashes by step against Bach's 51.9. Its dissonance is denser and
   far more accented (39.8 percent of clashes on a strong beat against 21.3), and it is prepared. That
   is exactly the thing the gate should permit and the critic cannot hear.

## What was measured, and what the numbers are not

Every work is read as one monophonic line per part. A melodic interval is the signed semitone step
between consecutive notes inside one voice. Shares are pooled across the corpus, and each share is also
reported per work as a band (mean, lowest work, highest work) so a reader can see the spread the band
hides. Devices and unexplained dissonances come from `tools/counterpoint/rules.py` profile `free`, the
same checker that grades a shipped cue, so a generator target and a gate number are the same
measurement and cannot drift apart.

Four limits are stated rather than buried:

- **Cadence detection is a chord pair read on a beat grid, and it is a floor, not a count.** A chord must
  last half a beat, the resolution must land on a beat, and cadences must be two beats apart, which
  stops a sequence of fifths from reading as six cadences. Where a suspension or a pedal hides the bass
  the reader undercounts. The accuracy check that goes with it is printed beside the per 100 bar rate:
  the labeller names the last chord of a work the tonic in 30 of the 35 works, so on the clearest
  cadence in each piece it is right 85.7 percent of the time and the per 100 bar rate is a lower bound.
- **The chord labeller is a template matcher with a quality prior, not a theory engine.** On the dense
  Harmony of Dissonance texture 42.4 percent of beats get no label at all, so its chord vocabulary
  statistics describe the labelled 57.6 percent and nothing more. The labels that are produced are
  readable (dominant sevenths, half diminished sevenths, diminished triads) and they are counted, not
  interpreted.
- **The clash census is bass relative and counts every voice.** A seven-voice arrangement will always
  show more clashes against its bass than a two-voice invention, so the Bach to HoD comparison is a
  comparison of written styles, not a like for like density test at equal voice counts. The per 100
  notes normalisation reduces but does not remove that bias; the placement and resolution columns are
  the ones that survive it.
- **Nothing melodic is stored, printed or committed.** Every field in both JSON files is a count or a
  share. The Harmony of Dissonance set is fan sequenced MIDI, measured only, never committed, never
  quoted, never used as generator input.

## 3. How M1b should build to these numbers

`data/counterpoint_targets.json` carries each target as a band: mean, lowest work, highest work.
The instruction to the generator is written here, because a band alone does not say how close is close.

| feature | target band (mean, low, high) | what a generator should do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| steps | 0.585, 0.189, 0.800 | stay inside 0.45 to 0.70 in a cue of 200 to 400 notes; a single work proves nothing at 0.19, the corpus median is what matters |
| leaps | 0.415, 0.200, 0.811 | the complement of steps; do not tune it separately |
| large leaps (7 semitones or more) | 0.148, 0.053, 0.232 | keep under 0.20. This is the feature that separates a Baroque line from a fanfare |
| leap recovery | 0.316, 0.040, 0.712 | at least 0.25. Recovery after a leap is the cheapest single rule that makes a line sing |
| notes inside a sequence | 36.95 per 100 notes | one sequence episode of 6 to 10 notes every 8 bars is the floor, not the ceiling |
| cadences | 11.04 per 100 bars | one cadence every 7 to 10 bars, and at least one perfect or imperfect authentic at the end |
| passing tones | 4.75 per 100 notes | this is the commonest device by a factor of two; a passage with none is not Baroque |
| appoggiaturas | 2.41 per 100 notes | the plan says sparingly; the corpus says twice in 100 notes, so sparingly is not never |
| suspensions | 0.89 per 100 notes | roughly one per 112 notes, which is one per 8 to 12 bars at these densities |
| unexplained dissonance | 0.35 per 100 notes | this is the failures-to-clean column, already calibrated in `tools/counterpoint/calibration.json` |
| imitation offset | 8, 6, 3, 2 beats | the answer enters between half a bar and two bars; a canon at four bars is not what the corpus does |

Why bands and not single values: the spread across 35 works is wide (steps run from 0.19 to 0.80),
because Bach writes both a singing invention and an arpeggiated one. A generator that hits the mean of
every feature will sound like a statistical average, which is the failure mode this whole lane exists to
avoid. The targets are a fence, not a destination.

## 4. Harmony of Dissonance: the dissonances that sound intentional, written as rules

The study set is 16 fan sequenced MIDI files of the soundtrack. They are measured, and nothing else:
not committed, not quoted, not used as generator input. The numbers in section 2 answer Nayil's question
in a form a checker can use.

What the set does, in one paragraph. It clashes roughly six times as often per note as Bach, on six
times as many beats, and it puts nearly twice the share of its clashes on a strong beat. Yet it
resolves slightly more of them by step than Bach does, and 65.5 percent of the dissonances the checker
judges are prepared. So the harmony is not careless; it is chromatic, dense, and heavily accented, with
the resolution doing the work. That is the bargain HOD-01 to HOD-06 encode: pay for the accent with the
resolution, and refuse the clash that neither resolves nor belongs to a labelled chord.

The six rules are in `data/hod_dissonance_devices.json`, each with its test, its measured basis and the
failure it prevents. In one line each:

- **HOD-01** a diminished seventh is colour on a weak beat when the voices enter and leave by step.
- **HOD-02** a semitone bass arrival on the tonic is a cadence colour, not an error, when the upper
  voice holds or steps and the chromatic note is a beat or shorter.
- **HOD-03** a tritone is an interval of the chord when both pitch classes belong to the label.
- **HOD-04** an accented second or seventh is legal when it resolves by step within an eighth note,
  because 51 percent of the measured clashes sit on a strong or medium beat and 56.8 percent of the
  voices that make one resolve by step, so the accent cannot be the test on its own.
- **HOD-05** constant doubling in octaves inside a phrase reads as one part in two registers, which is
  a registration choice on the 2A03 as much as on the organ.
- **HOD-06** one unprepared accented clash per phrase is allowed at a cadence, and two in four bars is
  not.

They are written as tests that narrow the existing fault, not as permissions that widen it: every one of
them requires a resolution or a chord membership that the current `rules.py` would otherwise fail. M1a
decides which enter `tools/counterpoint/rules.py`. `data/dissonance_rules.json` is a thirty device
contract with its own checker (`tools/check_dissonance_rules.py`, which requires exactly DIS-01 to
DIS-30) and this card does not edit it.

Standard pricing with a cached system prompt, so a hundred candidate cues is a rounding error against
the 150 USD budget. It is not queued anywhere, because it needs Nayil to resolve the billing block first.

## 6. The story for the Meta tooling score (25 of the 100 points)

`knowledge/WIN-PRIORITIES.md` records the weighting: Impact 30, Meta AI tooling 25, technical depth 20,
demo 25. The tooling score is not won by calling a model; it is won by showing a model inside a loop
that a judge can inspect. The loop this work makes possible:

1. **Propose.** A model writes four bars of ABC against a period, composer and instrumentation prompt.
   NotaGen does this symbolically, Muse Spark can do it in text, and both produce exactly one artifact:
   a candidate subject.
2. **Judge.** `tools/counterpoint/rules.py` grades the candidate against the corpus bands in section 3
   and the calibration in `tools/counterpoint/calibration.json`, and prints a clash rate per 100 notes
   beside the budget Bach's own maximum sets.
3. **Repair.** `tools/counterpoint/repair.py` fixes what the checker names, and the checker is re run so
   the before and after numbers are both measured, not asserted.
4. **Play.** The repaired line is arranged and rendered by the existing pipeline, which is the same
   pipeline the shipped cues use, so nothing in the demo is a mock.

What to put in front of a judge, concretely:

- one figure in the README: clashes per 100 notes before repair, after repair, and the corpus budget in
  the same units, with the command that produced all three columns;
- one 20 second clip where the repaired cue plays, because the ear is the final judge and the ear critic
  already scored the unrepaired title theme highest (which is the reason this lane exists);
- one honest line about the fallback: when the model is unavailable the in repo generator writes the
  subject, so the game never depends on an API key.

What not to say: that the model composes the score. It proposes; the rules decide. The Meta tooling
score rewards a real integration with a measurable effect, and the measurable effect here is the clash
rate falling while the cue keeps its character, which is a number a judge can re run.
