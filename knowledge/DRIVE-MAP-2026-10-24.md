## D18. The drive map draws in `drive_night`, transcribed from the sheet's own table. That is the answer to the palette question for the map.

`t_f034f9d0` asks whether the drive is a graded, non-NES look or a quantized one. Measured for this card:

* The drive's **tile art is already legal**: `assets/tiles/drive/drive_tiles.png` is 24 tiles, every pixel a Ricoh
  2C02 output, 9 colours, at most 3 non backdrop colours in every 16 by 16 area, and `tools/nes_check.py` passes it
  as a background. `tests/test_make_drive_tiles.py` is the file that proves it, not this card.
* `assets/tiles/drive/TILES.md` prints the four background palettes (`bg0_asphalt`, `bg1_verge`, `bg2_sign`,
  `bg3_lamp`) and the palette.json role behind every sheet role, so the set is **transcribed**, never chosen.
* The twelve 768 by 720 frames the audit measured (`output/drive/pp_drive_shot_01..12.png`) are **not** the map's
  art and are not drawn from a room palette at all: two of the three dominant colours in shot 01, `08131F` and
  `1B1B22`, are in neither `data/palette.json` nor `data/nes_palette.json` (grepped, 0 hits in both), so those
  frames cannot be the output of any room set this project can draw in.

**Ruling.** The drive map is quantized by construction: it draws the drive tile sheet, whose pixels are 2C02, in
`drive_night`, whose roles are the sheet's own. RULE 0 point 1 of the art bible is satisfied for this map by a set
written in `data/room_palettes.json`, and no exemption is claimed for the folder. What the twelve graded frames mean
for the drive **room** is a separate question with its own owner (R21a, `pp_drive.gml`, and card `t_f034f9d0`): the
room's placeholders draw from the `ui` set and this card does not touch them, because `pp_drive.gml` asks for
`window`, `window_shadow`, `window_edge`, `marker`, `marker_trail`, `accent` and `hazard`, which a road palette has
no business carrying.

The set, in the order it is written (by the role's index in `data/palette.json`):

    ink 0, paper 1, dim 3, text 5, grey_1 1, grey_3 3, grey_5 5,
    moss_1 25, moss_2 26, moss_3 27, moss_5 29, stone_2 32, gold_5 41

13 roles, 10 distinct colours, so 11 on a screen that has to spend the missing colour. `moss_3` is declared and not
used by this row: it is the gantry sign's face, and the sign is part of the corridor even though this row places no
gantry tile (see D19).

## D19. The two numbers: 160 wide, 24 tall, road on row 8.

Every number is forced by the scene's own commands, not by taste.

**Width.** The camera targets in the block are `round(camera_x * 0.15) mod 512` resolved through L3's 0.15 speed
(`LAYERS` in `tools/art/make_title_world.py`): shots 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 11 at camera tile 0 to 37, shot 7 at 71 to 83,
shot 8 at 133 to 141, shot 10 at 0 to 25. The furthest is 141, and a 16 tile frame from there needs the map to hold
tile 156, so the floor is 158 and this row uses **160**. `160 * 16 - 256 = 2304 px` is tile 144, so nothing clamps.

**Height.** A camera at tile y 8 shows rows 8 to 22 of a 240 px frame (`PP_FRAME_H`), which is 23 rows, and the map
holds **24** so the clamp (`24 * 16 - 240 = 144 px`, tile 9) sits past the row the scene asks for.

**Road row.** 8, the y all 15 of the scene's camera commands use and the y of its `start`.

**Consequence, stated so nobody is surprised by it:** with the camera's top row on the road row, the film's visible
band is the road at the top of the frame and the near verge under it, and rows 0 to 7 (the far hedge and verge) are
never in shot at that camera row. That is what the scene's own numbers do, not a layout choice this card made.

The band, top to bottom, and the tiles it uses (characters are the sheet's own column in `TILES.md` and
`drive_tiles.json`, not invented here):

    rows 0-1    hedge_night k            the far side, blocked
    row  2      verge_shadow l
    rows 3-4    verge_grass i, verge_tuft j
    row  5      kerb m
    row  6      road_shoulder e

    row  11     road_edge_line d

19 of the sheet's 24 characters; the five not placed are the gantry and the sign, which are film sprites drawn by
`pp_drive.gml` and not ground. The collision grid is computed from `data/tile_classes_drive.json`, the file the card
names as the walkable classification, so a reclassification moves the grid with it and no second table is kept in
step. 2,391 walkable cells, 1,449 blocked, and every walkable cell is reachable from `start`: the four openings in
the barrier exist so the near verge is not a second room with no way in.

## D20. The cue stays PP-02.

`title_theme` is neither a cue id nor a sound id in `data/audio.json`, which is why the block names `PP-02`.
`knowledge/DRIVE-SHOT-LIST.md` section 1 still says `title_theme`; its own Q2 records that the cue was renumbered,

shot list's prose is the stale side of that pair, not the block. No change to the block.

## What this card did not do, and the card that now owns it

The row names `tileset: drive_tiles`, and the game does not draw that sheet yet. `pp_map_tile_set_for_room` knows
`quay_night`, `office_night` and everything else as the ship, so a room palette called `drive_night` currently
resolves to the ship's table and the drive row draws the ship's deck tile in every cell. `tools/sync_art.py` also
does not read `assets/tiles/drive`, so the sheet is in no build index (`grep -c drive_tiles
game/datafiles/art/title_manifest.json` reads 0; `ts_ship` reads 1).

That is the same three-step shape the quay took (R46 tiles, ARRIVAL-MAP-2 map, R28 wire), and the wire is a card of
its own: a GML table for 24 characters, a source folder in `sync_art.py`, a `.yyp` regeneration, and a wire test in
the shape of `tests/test_quay_tiles_wire.py`. This card creates it rather than growing into it. Until it lands, the
honest statement is: the drive map is legal data, and the art that data names is not wired.
