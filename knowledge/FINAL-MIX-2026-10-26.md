## 2. Why a gain alone could not do it, and what the pass did

The four sources were 2.91 to 4.53 LU under the target with true peaks at -0.72 to -0.81 dBTP, so there was no
headroom to gain into: a plain gain of +4.53 dB puts the fight cue at +3.63 dBFS. The measured share of samples
above -1 dBFS after that gain is 0.11 percent on the drive, 0.09 on the harbor, 0.69 on the fight and 0.04 on the
boarding cue, so the work the limiter has to do is peak work, not body work. That is what the pass is:

1. decode the delivered OGG to 44.1 kHz mono float;
2. trim the render's edge fade and its silent tail off the loop region (section 3);
3. splice the loop tail into its head, only when the finished master's seam measures worse than clean;
4. gain, then a lookahead limiter (5 ms attack, 15 ms release, auto level disabled), iterated until the measured
   integrated loudness is inside 0.15 LU of minus 14 and the true peak is under the ceiling;
5. encode to Vorbis at quality 5, 44.1 kHz mono, and measure the OGG, not the WAV, because that is what ships.

One number matters more than the rest: **15 ms release**. The first build used 80 ms and cost 1.5 LU of loudness
range (5.3 to 3.0 on the fight cue) for no loudness a shorter release could not reach; at 15 ms the same cue lands
at -14.10 LUFS with its loudness range at 4.2. That build was moved out of the tree, not overwritten, and the
rebuild is the file that ships.

The limiter settings and every pass are in each folder's `_loop.json` sidecar. The four masters:

| Cue | Gain applied | Limiter | Passes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PP-02 | +3.77 dB | -2.20 dBFS | 5 |
| PP-03 | +2.96 dB | -1.30 dBFS | 2 |
| PP-04 | +5.29 dB | -1.60 dBFS | 4 |
| PP-17 | +3.57 dB | -1.30 dBFS | 2 |

A Vorbis decode overshoots its sample peak by about 0.8 dB here (measured: a -1.0 dBFS sample limiter came back at
-0.27 dBTP), which is why the limiter sits under the ceiling and why the ceiling is minus 0.5 dBTP rather than
minus 1.0. At minus 1.0 the same four cues need about 1.0 LU more limiting each, for 0.2 LU less loudness range,
which is the wrong trade for music that has to move.

## 3. The loop work, which was the real defect

`data/audio.json` loops these cues on the whole file, and three of the four files ended in a fade or in silence:

| Cue | What was at the loop end | Measured before | What the pass did | Measured after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PP-02 | level to the last sample | seam clean, wrap jump -54.96 dBFS | trimmed 2.0 ms of head and 0.16 ms of tail | clean, wrap -17.33 dBFS |
| PP-03 | a 77 ms fade to minus 64 dBFS | seam a level hole, tail RMS -64.02 dBFS | trimmed 76.6 ms of tail, 30 ms splice | clean, level -1.43 dB |
| PP-04 | 39 ms of digital silence | seam a level hole, tail RMS -240 dBFS | trimmed 39.4 ms of tail | clean, level -2.64 dB |
| PP-17 | 1518 ms of digital silence | seam a level hole, tail RMS -240 dBFS | trimmed 1518.3 ms of tail | clean, level -3.04 dB |

The rules, stated so a later card can re-run them:

- **trim**: walk 1 ms blocks from each end and drop every block more than 20 dB under the file's own RMS. That is a
  fade or silence, not music. The file length does not change, so a scene that plays the whole cue is unaffected;
  only the wrap moves.
- **splice**: an equal power crossfade of the loop's last 30 ms into its first 30 ms, applied only when the built
  master's seam measures worse than clean, and rebuilt from scratch when it is. 30 ms is shorter than a sixteenth
  at 126 bpm (119 ms), so no beat is displaced, and both halves of the crossfade are the cue's own audio.
- **seam error**: the wrap jump |x[start] - x[end-1]| in dBFS, judged against p99.9 of |x[i+1] - x[i]| over the
  file, which is the largest ordinary sample step the file already takes; plus the RMS of the 10 ms after the loop
  start and the 10 ms before its end, against the file's own RMS. `clean` means the wrap is not an outlier and the
  level does not step by more than 6 dB. A `dip` is a hole, a `click` is a jump outlier.

PP-03 is the one cue that needed the splice, and it needed it only after the master was measured: the trimmed
seam read as a level step in the first round, the tool rebuilt with the splice, and the second round measured
clean. That is the loop the tool exists for.
