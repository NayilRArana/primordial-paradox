## What this card did, and what it did not do

Four GPU batches were planned and two have run: batch 1 ZEUS-8DIR and batch 2 CAST, one kernel at a time,
eight candidates per asset, two kept per asset. Batches 3 and 4 are not measured because the shared queue is
booked by another card: their sections below say NOT MEASURED and the budget table gives their status.
Everything here is in the style the game already uses: this card changes no palette file, no shipped asset
and no prompt template.
`prep_r14_rows.positive()` and the palette-word path are R17's, unchanged. What is new is the row table, the
assets, and a desktop quantiser that removes the drawing's own backdrop before it quantises, which R17 did
not do (its cells were full squares of a downscaled drawing).

Nothing was pushed, published or uploaded. No dataset was touched. `game/`, `data/palette.json`,
`data/scene_palettes.json` and `data/audio.json` are unmodified; the only repository files this card writes
are its own new scripts, `data/sprite_redraw_queue.json` candidate rows, `output/art_candidates_r18/` and
this page. The candidate PNGs are not in any commit: `output/*` is gitignored in this project, as it is for
the R17 tree, so the durable record of them is `r18_checks.json`, the two `kept_manifest.json` files here and
the numbers on this page.

## The scorer, and the one thing it could not be

The card names the Gemini eye critic (`tools/eye_critic.py`) as the scorer. It could not run in this
worker: `python tools/eye_critic.py --dry-run` prints `key: absent from GEMINI_API_KEY, a real run would
refuse`, and the last four rows of `data/eye_ledger.csv` (2026-10-22) are `rate_limited`. So there is no eye
score in this page and none is invented. The ranking is a deterministic score over the same four tests the
critic grades, every component printed:

    silhouette  35 percent   one connected figure, 5 to 80 percent of the cell opaque, best near 42 percent
    outline     25 percent   the 1 px edge is the row's darkest entry and is not clipped at the cell border
    colour      20 percent   mean delta E of the mapping into the row, 0 at 0 dE and 0 at 10 dE or worse
    orphans     20 percent   no opaque pixel without an opaque neighbour, after the cleanup pass

A human eye still has the last word: the contact sheet per batch is in `output/art_candidates_r18/`.

20 of 384 pixels, which is 5 percent, so it is one pose drawn sixteen times with a wobble. Cycle 143 differs
by about 176 pixels between frames, which is not a step either, it is a different drawing every time. The
deterministic score cannot see this, because silhouette, outline, orphan pixels and palette are all measured
on a single frame. So the two kept cycles are the best sixteen still frames, not two usable walks, and this
card did not draw a usable 8 direction walk. That is the finding the next card needs, and it is why the
candidates are queued rather than promoted.

## Open questions this card leaves to gamedev

1. Which of the two kept candidates per asset is the one to draw from. This card ranks; it does not pick.
2. Whether a 32x32 battle portrait may spend a fourth palette row. A 32x32 cell is 4x4 tiles, so it can
   carry four rows legally where a 48x48 face cannot; this card holds it to one row so the cell reads as
   one figure, which is the stricter reading.
3. Whether a 24x24 slash frame sits on a 16x24 character cell, or the slash belongs to the 16x16 weapon
   cell that card t_503707c8 is drawing against.
4. Whether scene plates belong in `data/sprite_redraw_queue.json` at all. This card put them there because
   the card asked for every candidate in the queue; a plate is not a sprite and the queue's own rule says
   one row per sprite the game asks for by name.
