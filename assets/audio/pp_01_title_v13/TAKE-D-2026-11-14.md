## 2. What changed in the music

The lead in two bars, and nothing else. Bars 13-14 and 23-24 are the device unit (`T_V1s` then
`T_V2`). Take C's device bar opened ON the peak and fell, and the second bar jumped back up to that
peak, so the two bar phrase changed direction twice and the hook meter refused to call it an arch:

    take C  bar 13  A5:6 G5:2 E5:4          then bar 14  A5 G5 E5 D5 C5 B4
    take D  bar 13  A4:2 C5:2 E5:2 G5:2 A5:4 then bar 14  A5 G5 E5 D5 C5 B4 (unchanged)

Take D climbs to the same peak and holds it a quarter at the top of the bar, so the whole phrase
rises and then falls once, on the way out. All 13 returns read arch or pedal now; take C had 11 of
13, the two that did not being these device statements.

The device's colour changes with it, and the change is a device `knowledge/MUSIC-DISSONANCE.md`
names: over the F in the bass, A4 is the third and C5 the fifth, and E5 and G5 are diatonic passing
tones between chord tones, entered by step and left by step, which is the preparation, dissonance,
resolution grammar of section 2 (clause S68). Take C's held A5 over the moving bass was a
suspension; take D spends that suspension on the quarter at the crest instead.

The refused rounds are the reason the bar starts on A4 rather than on a fifth or a seventh. A
phrase that begins on the fifth of F puts the lead a fifth above the bass on beat 1 and walks it up
with the bass, which is a parallel fifth; a phrase that begins on the seventh sounds the major
seventh against the chord on a strong beat, which the gate counts as a raw clash. Starting on the
third leaves the sixth above the bass on beat 1 and steps up inside the chord.
