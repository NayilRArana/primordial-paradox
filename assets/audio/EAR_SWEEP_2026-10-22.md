# Gemini ear sweep, 2026-10-22

Card GEMINI-EAR-SWEEP. Every rendered theme in this folder was pushed through the fixed
six question ear in `tools/ear_critic.py` (card MUS-EAR), and this file is the ledger: one row
per audio file, the best file per criterion, the changes the model repeats across renders, and
one listen order for Nayil.

Model reported by the tool in every review header: `gemini-3.5-flash`. Rubric: the fixed six
1 to 10 scores (hook, drums, tension, human feel, instrument variety, structure), in the same
order, every time.

Scope: every `.mp3` and `.ogg` under `assets/audio/`, 17 files, which are 9 distinct masters in
two codecs (`assets/audio/sfx/` excluded as the card says; the 24 effects there are one shots,
not themes). `title_theme/` (v1) is included even though the card lists v2 onward, because it is
a rendered title theme and leaving it out would have left a hole in the comparison.

## Run facts

- Calls: 12 files were scored in this sweep between 14:27 and 14:35 UTC; 5 more were scored
  earlier the same day (06:38 to 06:51 UTC) by the same tool and model and are reused here with
  their original timestamps; 3 ogg siblings could not be scored because the free tier daily
  quota ran out mid sweep (detail below). Own measurement exists for 14 of 17 files, and the
  remaining 3 are codec siblings whose master is measured.
- Rate limit: the tool gates on its own ledger timestamps at 10 calls per minute. Densest
  rolling 60 second window in today's ledger: 7 recorded calls. The retries that came back 429
  are not appended as rows, so the true request rate was slightly above 7 and never above the
  10 per minute gate.
- Quota: at 14:35 UTC the free tier answered 429 with `limit: 20` requests per day for the
  flash model, and asked for a retry in roughly 16 hours. The three files that lost their run
  to it are listed in the table as `carried`.
- Key handling: `GEMINI_API_KEY` was read at call time only (process environment, filled from
  the Windows user environment when the shell had started before it was set), never printed,
  never written. A scan of the ledger, all 21 review files and the run logs for the key value
  and for the standard Google API key prefix found nothing.

## What the ear's own noise floor is

Before trusting any gap, the same audio was scored more than once:

- 12 comparisons of the same audio (repeat runs of the same file, and mp3 against ogg of the
  same master). Largest difference on any criterion in any comparison: 1 point.
- The worst case, `title_theme_v3.mp3` against `title_theme_v3.ogg`, differed by exactly 1 point
  on all six criteria at once (29 against 35 total).
- `title_theme_v4_medium` scored identically twice at 06:40:58 and 06:51:23, then 1 point lower
  on variety and structure at 14:34:29.

So: a gap of 1 point per criterion is noise, 2 or more is a real difference, and comparing file
totals within 3 points is meaningless. That rules out ranking v4_medium (36.0 pooled mean),
v4_subtle (35.5) and hadrian (35.0) against each other on ear scores alone.

## The top 5 changes the ear repeats across files

Counted over the five concrete changes and the two mechanical moments in all 9 masters (v1, v2,
v3, v3_126, v4_medium, v4_spicy, v4_subtle, percy, hadrian). Bar numbers are the ear's own and
it does miscount bars, so read them as "roughly this spot".

1. **Delayed vibrato and pitch slides on held lead notes. 9 of 9 masters, often item 1 or 2.**
   Nearly the same wording each time: a vibrato that starts a few frames after the note attack,
   plus a short pitch slide into the note, so a dotted half note stops sounding like flat
   square wave. Spots it names: v1 bars 13 to 16, v2 bars 5 to 8, v3 bars 25 to 28, v3_126 bars
   19 to 34, v4_medium bars 19 to 20, v4_spicy bars 9 to 10, v4_subtle bar 23, percy bars 13 to
   16, hadrian bars 7 to 8. This is one arranger feature, not nine problems: the arranger writes
   sustained lead notes with no pitch envelope.
2. **Humanize note lengths and volume, not just timing. 9 of 9.** Every "most mechanical moment"
   section names identical gate lengths, flat velocity, instant note off and no micro timing.
   The changes it asks for are a 1 to 2 tick delay on alternating notes, a short volume decay on
   offbeats, and duty cycle variation. Same root cause in all nine.
3. **Give the drums something to say. 9 of 9.** The complaint is identical across the waltz
   renders: the noise channel holds one boom, chick, chick figure for the whole track. What it
   asks for: a 16th note snare fill or roll into the next section (v1, v2, v3, v3_126,
   v4_medium, v4_spicy, v4_subtle, percy, hadrian all have one), plus ghost notes, accent
   variation and a swing on offbeats. This is the single change that appears in every file's
   top five and it is also the criterion the ear scores lowest after human feel (mean 4.36).
4. **Move the bass: pedal point or a descending line, never static roots. 9 of 9.** Six masters
   are told to sit on a sustained low tonic pedal under a moving lead (v3 bars 17 to 24,
   v3_126 bars 27 to 28, v4_medium bars 25 to 27, percy bars 9 to 12, hadrian bars 17 to 20,
   v4_spicy bars 29 to 32 with a chromatic descent), and three are told to replace static root
   quarter notes with a stepwise descending walking line (v1 bars 15 to 16, v2 bars 29 to 32,
   v4_subtle bars 17 to 22).
5. **Leave a hole before the reprise or the loop. 7 of 9.** A one or two bar drop-out or mute
   right before the full ensemble comes back: v1 bar 24, v2 bars 49 to 50, v3_126 bar 33 (snare
   mute then a rising roll), v4_spicy bars 49 to 50, v4_subtle bar 36, percy bar 32, hadrian bar
   24. The ear reads the rest as the thing that makes the return feel like a return.

A sixth, close behind at 6 of 9, if Nayil wants six: raise the harmonic tension in the bar
before the turnaround with an altered dominant or a chromatic substitution (it names a sharp
five, sharp nine or flat nine colouring, a diminished substitution, and add9 style extensions),
in v2, v3, v3_126, v4_medium, percy, and v1 in the weaker form of a reharmonised pedal.

Note on how to read this list: the ear is a rough listener that hears the mix, not the score.
The value here is not that any single bar number is right. It is that four of the five items are
one arranger rule each (pitch envelope on held leads, gate and velocity variation, drum fill
before a section change, bass motion under the lead), and rules can be implemented once and
applied to every render.

## How to check this file

- Row count: 17 rows for 17 files. `ls assets/audio/*/*.mp3 assets/audio/*/*.ogg` returns the
  same 17 names; `sfx/` holds only `.ogg` and `.wav` one shots, excluded by the card.
- Raw call log: `python tools/ear_critic.py --ledger` prints 45 rows for today; 43 `ok`, 2
  `superseded` from the earlier card run. New review files: `ls -t assets/audio/ear_reviews/`.
- Rate limit: rolling window over ledger timestamps, maximum 7 recorded calls in 60 s, gate at 10.
- No key: the ledger, all review files and the run logs were scanned for the key value and for
  the standard Google API key prefix with a script that compares in memory and prints only
  booleans. Zero hits.
- Scores are reproducible only within the 1 point noise floor above; do not read a 1 point
  difference as a finding.
